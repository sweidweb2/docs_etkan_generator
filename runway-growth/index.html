ETKAN Growth Runway 01

Executive Summary

Mission ETKAN is a "parauniversity" giving students a real, hands-on engineering experience - currently in mechatronics/industrial engineering, with a software (web development & agentic AI) track planned for a later stage.

The Problem Students in Lebanon aged 16-21 have limited access to real engineering experience, market-relevant instruction, or a network built around actual industry practice.

The Solution A weekly-class + 5-hour weekly task learning path where instructors - all with real market/industry experience - direct students to apply what they learn rather than spend time on basic explanation. Each team builds a ready-to-market MVP over the program.

The Studio Each team's MVP feeds into ETKAN's studio: with a market study supplied by an accelerator-investor partner, the strongest MVPs become real startups, co-founded by ETKAN and the investor. Graduating students choose either a technical role in the resulting company or an equity stake with management responsibility. Semester fees cover the cost of running the learning path - the studio is where ETKAN actually captures upside, turning the education program into a venture pipeline rather than a standalone tuition business.

Services

  • ETKAN Learning Path - main service, 4-semester program
  • Third-party courses and workshops services

Value Proposition

  • Real engineering experience unavailable elsewhere in Lebanon for this age group
  • A path to employment, a startup, or an instructor role - not just a certificate
  • No direct competitor combines a sustained multi-semester path with a real MVP/startup outcome for 16-21 year olds

Competitor Landscape

Mechatronics/Engineering track (current):

Direct comparables:

  • RobotX Academy (robotics/coding, ages 5-18, Beirut),
  • LAU Robotics & VR Summer School (short-format, middle-school-skewed),
  • World Robot Olympiad Lebanon (annual competition, ages 6-22),
  • Geek Express / The Little Engineer / Origami Academy (broader STEAM camps, shorter format)

Indirect/downstream: LAU (only full Mechatronics bachelor's in Lebanon), AUB/BAU (mechatronics as a mechanical-engineering track), Balamand, RHU, AUST - 4-year commitment, not competing for the pre-university window

Gap held: no comparable runs a sustained, multi-semester, real-experience program ending in an MVP/startup outcome for 16-21 year olds

Software (Web Dev & AI) track - for later-stage launch:

Tier 1, highest threat - 42 Beirut: self-described "tech para-university," project-based/peer-to-peer/no-teacher model, fully tuition-free (funded by CMA CGM), ~150 students/year, no degree prerequisite. Global 42 model typically requires 18+ - worth confirming Beirut's actual minimum age, since that determines real overlap with ETKAN's 16-21 band. Nearly identical positioning language to ETKAN's own ("para-university," real-world/project-based) - this is the one competitor that should get its own line in any investor conversation.

Tier 2, adult-oriented bootcamps:

  • SE Factory (14-week full-stack, ~90% placement),
  • Holberton School Beirut (ML/AR-VR/full-stack, income-share tuition, LAU partnership),
  • Codi (6-month full-stack, laptops + transport included),
  • Beirut AI/Zaka (AI, requires existing programming skill),
  • LIT,
  • Code Fellows Beirut (92% employment),
  • Nucamp.

Lebanon's tech workforce skews 18-34 - none of these target teenagers specifically, which remains ETKAN's structural advantage in this tier.

2. PR - University Workshops

Minimum 4 workshops, run at partner universities (LU,USAL,MU,UA, LAU, AUB)

Why: These are the exact schools training ETKAN's indirect competitors' students. Their engineering clubs supply the room and audience for free, and their students are already engineering-adjacent - higher odds of converting into referrals.

Target math

4 workshops x ~40-60 attendees = 150-240 leads - this is what turns the Stage 1 "150 students" milestone into an actual funnel instead of a hoped-for number.
3. Marketing

ETKAN's awareness strategy centers on one positioning line - a 4-semester path that ends in a real product, run by instructors who've actually built one - split across two audiences on two channels: students (16-21) via Instagram/TikTok, driven by concrete outcomes (MVP, job, teaching role) rather than generic "hands-on learning" claims; and parents, who fund the $300/semester fee, via Facebook/WhatsApp, driven by instructor credibility and program structure rather than hype. Every channel routes to the same low-commitment CTA - reserve a spot at an info session or lab visit - rather than a direct signup, since a semester-long paid commitment converts on trust-building, not impulse. Marketing runs in tandem with the PR university-workshop track (Section 2), which supplies the bulk of Stage 1's qualified leads at effectively zero acquisition cost; paid awareness spend exists to build brand recognition around that pipeline, not to replace it.
