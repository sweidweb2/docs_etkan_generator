About ETKAN

ETKAN is a Lebanon-based paraeducation program in mechatronics and industrial engineering, built to give students the real engineering experience that a purely academic track cannot provide on its own. Our instructors bring direct market and industry experience into the room, and our programs are designed around one core conviction: engineering students should graduate knowing not just how to analyze a problem, but how real engineering teams actually work.

This workshop is offered as a standalone session for engineering faculties who want to give their students an early, honest look at that gap — and how to close it before graduation.

The Problem

University curricula build essential theoretical foundations and analytical rigor. But most engineering students in Lebanon reach graduation without a clear picture of how that theory is actually used inside a company: how decisions get made, how time and budget constrain design, and how much of real engineering work is selection and integration rather than derivation from first principles.

The result is a blind spot — not in what students know, but in how they’ve been trained to think about applying it. “The Engineering Blind Spot” workshop exists to name that gap directly and give students a practical mental model for closing it.

Executive Summary

While university education builds the critical theoretical foundation and analytical rigor necessary for engineering, students often lack context on how these tools operate within actual industrial workflows.

This workshop addresses the shift from academic problem-solving (deriving from first principles) to industrial problem-solving (selection, constraint management, software proficiency limits, and alignment with company Standard Operating Procedures) — what we call the shift from an analysis mentality to a selection mentality.

Format
1 session · 60–90 min
Audience
Engineering students, all majors

Key Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, students will be able to:

Optimize Tool Mastery Understand the 80/20 rule of engineering tools — balancing software proficiency, calculations, and Design for Manufacturing (DFM/DFA).
Shift from Derivation to Selection Recognize when engineering requires original design versus when it demands component selection, system integration, and configuration.
Understand Corporate Workflows Learn how multi-disciplinary teams function within real-world Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).
Strategize University Years Differentiate between research paths and industry paths early, to build a targeted portfolio and industrial network.
