## 1. What ETKAN is

ETKAN is a **Lebanon-based parauniversity / paraeducation** focused on **mechatronics and industrial engineering** (with a later software / web / agentic AI track planned).

Official self-descriptions used across docs:

| Label used | Context |
|---|---|
| Parauniversity | Growth runway, structural overview, student regulations |
| Paraeducation | Engineering Blind Spot workshop |
| Applied Industrial Engineering Parauniversity | Student System cover |
| Applied Industrial Engineering Academy | 3D Printing for Educators CTA |
| Productive educational learning path | Website / portfolio blurbs |

**Core conviction:** students should get a **real engineering experience**, not classroom theory alone — knowing not only how to analyze a problem, but how real engineering teams actually work (selection, constraints, SOPs, integration).

Structurally it is **two businesses in one**:

1. **The School** — education arm (break-even / opex coverage via fees)
2. **The Studio** — startup studio (equity upside from converting student MVPs into companies)

## 2. Mission, problem, solution

### Mission
Give students a real, hands-on engineering experience — currently in mechatronics / industrial engineering; later a software track (web development & agentic AI).

### Problem
Students in Lebanon aged **16–21** (marketing also reaches from **9th grade+** through first-year university) have limited access to:

- Real engineering experience  
- Market-relevant instruction  
- A network built around actual industry practice  

University curricula build theory and analytical rigor, but many graduates lack context on how theory is used inside companies: decisions under time/budget, selection vs derivation, software proficiency limits, and company SOPs. ETKAN names this **“The Engineering Blind Spot.”**

### Solution
A sustained multi-semester **weekly class + ~5 hours/week of tasks** learning path, run by instructors with **real market/industry experience**, where instructors **direct and leverage** learning rather than re-teach basics. Over the program, each team builds a **market-ready MVP**. Strongest MVPs can feed into the Studio with investor-backed market studies.

### Value proposition (stated)
- Real engineering experience unavailable elsewhere in Lebanon for this age group  
- A path to **employment**, a **startup**, or an **instructor role** — not just a certificate  
- No direct competitor combines a sustained multi-semester path with a real MVP/startup outcome for 16–21 year olds

## 6. Delivery model (how teaching works)

### Learning Path delivery
- **Weekly class** — live, **in-person** (≈ **2 hours** per Student System)  
- **~5 hrs/week tasks** — learn a topic → build a project → engineering design practice tied to class  
- Tasks mostly remote; **hardware-dependent tasks happen in the lab**  
- Instructors **direct and leverage** learning (not re-teach basics)  
- All instructors have **real market experience**  
- No class recordings  
- Materials for educational use only  
- Practice-first pedagogy: guided thinking in class, self-driven study between sessions, practical work in the lab  

### Evaluation
- Tasks/assignments mandatory; non-submit → usually zero  
- Late submissions at teacher discretion (full or reduced grade)  
- Larger/final project may count  
- Teacher evaluation; optional final exam by course  
- Overall phase performance per course/phase system  

### Certificates
1. **Certificate of Participation** — attended; does **not** confirm successful completion  
2. **Certificate of Completion** — requirements successfully met  

### AI / tools policy
- AI tools allowed only when teacher permits  
- Students must understand and explain their work

## 7. Student system & regulations

Official document for all registered Learning Path students (EN + AR). Key operating rules:

### Attendance
- Mandatory  
- Justified absences only with teacher approval  
- Justified absences ≤ **30%** of scheduled sessions (e.g. 12 sessions → max 4)  
- Unapproved absence not permitted  
- Lateness allowed but does not replace absence limit  

### Tuition (regulations — current operating price)
- Standard phase: **$350** total  
  - Initial **$100**  
  - Remaining **$250** over next **5 months** (default)  
- Other schedules possible with management  
- Generally **non-refundable**  
- Missed payments → management contacts student  

### Withdrawal
- Cannot pause active phase  
- Withdraw in first **2 months** → still responsible for **50%** of phase fee (**$175** of $350)  
- After 2 months → withdrawal not accepted; installments remain due even if attendance stops  
- Returning later: class availability, level assessment/placement, management approval  

### Financial aid
- Requests handled by management; subject to conditions/availability  
- Aid does not change program requirements  

### Scheduling
- Set collaboratively (students, teachers, management)  
- Official agreed schedule takes precedence  

### Equipment & workspace
- Student must bring/maintain a suitable **laptop**  
- Workspace usable with management coordination; internet available  
- Parauniversity equipment needs prior approval  
- Cleanliness / respect for shared spaces required  

### Conduct
- Attend + complete tasks  
- No phone use during class  
- Respect teachers, management, peers  
- Professional learning environment  

### Communication
- Official via management  
- Course matters → teachers  
- Payments / admin / aid → management  
- Announcements: official **WhatsApp** group  
- Expected response within **24 hours**  

### IP & projects
- Student projects developed in the parauniversity are **open source**; students may share and build on them  
- ETKAN may **close** a project (stop/limit public sharing) by management decision  
- Management may showcase projects  
- Unpublished roadmaps / internal docs / learning resources stay internal — no redistribution without authorization  

### Acknowledgment
Registration = student confirms they read and agree to educational system, responsibilities, payment terms, confidentiality, and regulations.

## 10. The Studio — MVP → startup pipeline

Pipeline as documented:

1. Team builds MVP over the learning path using an **investor-validated market study** (from investor incubators / accelerator-investor partner)  
2. On completion, team chooses:  
   - (a) **technical employees** in the new startup, or  
   - (b) **equity + management responsibilities**  
3. **ETKAN takes shares AND manages** the resulting startup  
4. Investor provides **capital + market distribution**  
5. A **dedicated studio manager** (not the school’s general manager) runs founded startups  
6. ETKAN holds **control** over founded startups — **exact governance still undefined**  
7. ~**90% of teams won’t convert** to a company — accepted, because guaranteed student value is experience + network unavailable elsewhere in Lebanon  

**Economic logic:** tuition covers running the path; Studio is where ETKAN captures upside and turns education into a venture pipeline. Strongest MVPs become real startups **co-founded by ETKAN and the investor**.

## 16. Marketing, funnels & awareness

Source: Jalset marketing decks (AR + EN) — product is the **3-year industrial & manufacturing roadmap** aligned with ETKAN’s learning offer. Treat as ETKAN go-to-market strategy.

### Target audience
**9th grade+ → high school → first-year university**  
Dual decision-makers: **student (emotional)** + **parent (financial/trust)**.

### Core conversion path
1. Lead from social / awareness  
2. WhatsApp — qualify & trust  
3. Meeting — explain roadmap  
4. Onboard if fit  

**Separate entry:** free **Directing Workshop** for students who don’t know which domain/path to choose — sell **clarity** first, roadmap second.

### Funnel 1 — Already want technology
Social → interest → WhatsApp → meeting → roadmap → enrollment.  
Social sells **vision**, not tool lists (“one roadmap connecting programming, electronics, hardware, mechanics, AI”).

### Funnel 2 — Don’t know what they want
Free Directing Workshop → understand paths → discover fit → realize roadmap is an option → WhatsApp/consult → meeting → enrollment.

### Awareness levels (Eugene Schwartz-style)
| Level | Content job | Example themes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 No awareness | Spark curiosity | Manufacturing jobs 2035; smart vs traditional machines |
| 2 Problem awareness | Show the gap | One skill isn’t enough; random courses without roadmap |
| 3 Solution awareness | Introduce category | How programming + electronics + mechanics + AI work together |
| 4 Product awareness | Sell roadmap | Year 1/2/3; progression of domains |
| 5 Most aware | Remove doubt | Projects, testimonials, FAQs, pricing, who it’s for / isn’t |

### Dual messaging tracks
| Student | Parent |
|---|---|
| Future, excitement, identity, projects, tech, career | Structure, education, discipline, roadmap, outcomes, credibility, safety, investment |

### Sales method (“fishing rod”)
- Talk less; short curiosity question  
- WhatsApp: **3–5 lines max**; details in meeting/workshop  
- Parents: trust question before price  
- Curiosity → meeting → offer (don’t sell in first message)  

Example questions:
- “What’s the one thing that worries you most about your technical future?”  
- “If you had 3 years: 10 scattered courses or one connected roadmap?”  
- “Have you ever built something with your hands… or was it all on paper?”

## Appendix A — One-page ETKAN snapshot

**ETKAN** is a Beirut parauniversity for mechatronics/industrial engineering that runs a practice-first multi-semester learning path (weekly live class + weekly tasks + lab) for roughly ages 16–21, with parents as payers. Fees (~$300–350 per semester/phase) are meant to cover operations only. The real upside is a **Studio** that converts a minority of team MVPs into investor-backed startups where ETKAN takes equity and management control. Near-term plan: stand up lab/office, run university PR workshops, hit ~150 students in 4–5 months, then attempt first startup conversion while expanding. Biggest open risks: equity/governance design, studio leadership, single-investor dependence, and resolving public inconsistencies on program length and price.
