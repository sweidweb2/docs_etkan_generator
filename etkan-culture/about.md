ETKAN — Full Structural Overview
1. Identity
•	A "parauniversity" in Beirut, Lebanon, focused on mechatronics and industrial engineering
•	Mission: give students a real engineering experience, not classroom theory
•	Structurally two businesses in one: The School (education, break-even) + The Studio (startup studio, equity upside)
________________________________________
2. Services (3)
1.	ETKAN Learning Path — main service, 5-semester program
2.	Private tutoring — programming and engineering
3.	3rd-party workshop services
________________________________________
3. The Learning Path — Delivery Model
•	Weekly class + 5 hrs/week of tasks
•	Tasks = learn a topic → build a project → engineering design practice tied to class
•	Instructors direct and leverage learning (not re-teach basics) — all have real market experience
•	Classes are in-person/live; only tasks are remote
•	Hardware-dependent tasks happen in the lab
•	Over the full 5 semesters, each team builds a market-ready MVP, based on a market study supplied by the investor's incubators
________________________________________
4. Financials (Learning Path)
Item	Value
Fee	$300/semester × 2/year = $600/student/year
Team size	10 students
Revenue/team/year	~$6,000
Purpose of fees	Cover opex only (instructors, staff, lab) — not a profit driver
Real profit engine	Studio equity, not tuition
________________________________________
5. Capacity
Dimension	Capacity	Constraint
Instructors	200, up to 500	No extra training needed
Lab (hardware tasks)	15 total / 4 at a time	Hard physical limit
Remote (most tasks)	Up to ~60 students	Quality decreases as load increases — curve unmeasured
________________________________________
6. Quality & Automation (self-rated, unverified)
•	Learning path: 85/100 (lab-limited; could hit 95/100 with bigger lab)
•	Tutoring: 80/100 (instructors in training; ~90/100 expected after 1 month)
•	Workshops: same as tutoring
•	System automation: 85/100 — general management dept (sales funnel, finance) + instructor team/manager (teaching QC, student experience)
________________________________________
7. The Studio — MVP → Startup Pipeline
1.	Team builds MVP over 5 semesters using investor-validated market study
2.	On completion, team picks: (a) technical employees in the new startup, or (b) equity + management responsibilities
3.	ETKAN takes shares AND manages the resulting startup
4.	Investor provides capital + market distribution
5.	A dedicated studio manager (not the school's general manager) runs founded startups
6.	ETKAN holds control over founded startups — exact governance still undefined
7.	~90% of teams won't convert to a company — accepted as fine, since guaranteed value to students is experience + network unavailable elsewhere in Lebanon
________________________________________
8. Student Outcome Tracks (3 goals)
1.	Employment in an engineering position
2.	Open a startup (via the Studio)
3.	Become an instructor within ETKAN
(Unresolved: these three tracks compete for the same top-tier students — no stated allocation criteria)
________________________________________
9. Stages of Development
Stage	Focus
1	Run 3 core services; improve quality & automation; develop student-instructors
2	Expand across Beirut; begin sending students to employment
2 (parallel)	Launch first startup with the investor
3	Expand across all of Lebanon, multiple centers
4	Undefined — "next step"
(Flagged risk: Stage 2 bundles low-risk geographic execution with the highest-uncertainty, unproven mechanism — the first startup conversion)
________________________________________
10. Unresolved / Open Items (the ones that matter most for investors)
1.	Equity split — ETKAN↔investor and ETKAN↔student founders — not defined either side
2.	Studio operating leadership — dedicated manager planned but not named/resourced
3.	Founder vs. ETKAN control conflict — students offered "equity + management," but ETKAN also holds control; needs reconciliation before students are told
4.	Single-investor dependency — market study, capital, and distribution all run through one relationship, no fallback
5.	Stage sequencing risk — proven expansion and unproven Studio launch overlap in Stage 2
6.	Talent allocation — no criteria for who goes to employment vs. founding vs. teaching
7.	Remote quality-decay curve — not measured; no defined ceiling
________________________________________
This is the complete structure as it stands today — the School is a defined, break-even operation with real (if soft) capacity limits; the Studio is a promising but structurally incomplete mechanism where the two biggest gaps (equity split and operating leadership) are the same two things standing between "compelling story" and "fundable business."
