ETKAN Student System & Regulations 01

1. Introduction

This document explains how ETKAN operates: the structure of the program, what students can expect, and what is expected from them. It is the official Student System & Regulations for the parauniversity.

It applies to all students registered on the ETKAN learning path, including any course within a phase.

ETKAN is a productive educational learning path focused on hands-on training and specialized workshops. Learning is practice-first: guided thinking in class, self-driven study between sessions, and practical work in the lab.

By registering, each student is responsible for reading, understanding, and following these regulations.

2. Parauniversity Program Structure

The parauniversity is organized into phases. Each phase lasts approximately six (6) months and may contain one or more courses.

Courses in each phase follow the parauniversity’s published roadmap. For example, Intro to Mechatronics currently includes Python, Arduino, and Data & Computer. The roadmap is provided by the parauniversity and may be updated over time.

  • A student must successfully complete a phase before progressing to the next phase.
  • Students may take a break between phases.
  • Students cannot pause an active phase and resume it later.
  • Students who wish to enter at a later phase based on previous knowledge should discuss this with parauniversity management.

3. Classes & Learning Format

  • One class/session per week, approximately two (2) hours.
  • No class recordings are provided.
  • Educational materials are provided for the student’s educational use only.

4. Attendance

Attendance is mandatory. A student may be absent only with a justification approved by the teacher. Justified absences must not exceed 30% of the total scheduled sessions of the relevant phase or course (example: 12 sessions → up to 4 justified absences).

  • Absence without the teacher’s approval is not permitted.
  • Students may arrive late; lateness does not replace the limit above.
  • Attendance remains the student’s responsibility — the program is practical and progressive.

5. Tasks & Assignments

  • Each class includes a task/assignment. Tasks are mandatory and contribute to evaluation.
  • Failure to submit normally results in zero for that task.
  • Late submissions may be accepted at the teacher’s discretion, with full or reduced grade as the teacher decides.
  • Students are expected to complete tasks because later material builds on previous work.
  • A larger or final project may form part of the overall evaluation.

6. Evaluation & Student Progress

Evaluation consists primarily of tasks and assignments, a final project and/or examination where applicable, and the teacher’s evaluation.

A teacher may introduce a final exam depending on the course. Methods may differ between courses. At the end of the phase, overall performance is evaluated according to the applicable system for that course/phase.

Prior knowledge is not required when starting from the beginning. Students with prior knowledge may discuss placement with management.

7. Completion & Certificates

Certificate of Participation — confirms that the student participated in the phase/program. It does not necessarily indicate successful completion.

Certificate of Completion — issued when the student successfully completes the requirements of the phase or course.

Only a Certificate of Completion confirms that requirements were successfully met.

8. Tuition & Payment Structure

For a standard phase: total fee $350 — initial payment $100, remaining balance $250.

  • Default: remaining $250 distributed across the following five (5) months.
  • Another schedule may be agreed with management where applicable.
  • Payments are generally non-refundable.
  • Payment questions are handled through management. If a payment is missed, management will contact the student about the outstanding amount.

9. Withdrawal / Dropping a Phase

A student cannot pause an active phase. A break is only allowed between phases (Section 2). Dropping an active phase is different from that break.

  • A student may withdraw during the first two (2) months of the phase. In that period, the student remains responsible for 50% of the phase fee (50% of $350 = $175). Payments already made are non-refundable, and any remaining balance under that 50% stays due as agreed with management.
  • Withdrawal after the first two (2) months is not accepted. The student remains responsible for the installments even if they stop attending.
  • Stopping attendance does not cancel what is still due.
  • Returning later is subject to available classes, current level, parauniversity assessment/placement, and management approval.

10. Financial Aid & Scholarships

Students may request financial aid or scholarship consideration. Requests are handled by management, subject to parauniversity conditions and availability. Receiving assistance does not change the program requirements.

11. Scheduling

Schedule is set collaboratively among students, teachers, and management. Flexibility is available where reasonably possible. Students should raise scheduling problems with management. Once agreed, the official schedule takes precedence.

12. Equipment & Workspace

Student equipment. Each student must have a suitable laptop and is responsible for bringing and maintaining it.

Parauniversity workspace. Students may use the workspace with management coordination. Internet is available. Use of parauniversity equipment requires prior approval from management.

Workspace conduct. Maintain cleanliness and organization, respect shared equipment and spaces, and leave the workspace in appropriate condition.

13. Student Responsibilities & Conduct

  • Attend classes and complete assigned tasks.
  • Phone use during class is not allowed.
  • Respect teachers, management, and other students.
  • Maintain a professional learning environment and follow workspace rules.
  • Take responsibility for assigned work.

14. AI & External Tools

  • Students may use AI tools when permitted by the teacher.
  • Students must not use AI to complete assigned tasks unless the teacher explicitly allows it.
  • When AI use is permitted, the teacher may define the acceptable scope.
  • Students remain responsible for understanding and explaining their submitted work.

15. Communication & Support

  • Official parauniversity communication is handled through management.
  • Contact teachers directly for course-related matters.
  • Contact management for payments, scheduling, administration, financial aid, and other parauniversity matters.
  • Official announcements are shared in the parauniversity’s official WhatsApp group.
  • Expected response time: within 24 hours. Private communication with instructors is allowed when appropriate.

16. Student Projects & Intellectual Property

  • Projects developed as part of the parauniversity are open source. Students may share them and build on them.
  • ETKAN keeps the right to close a project — to stop or limit public sharing — when management decides.
  • Management may showcase student projects.
  • Roadmaps, internal documents, and learning resources that have not been released stay internal.
  • Students may not redistribute those internal materials, or give them to outside parties, without authorization.

17. Leaving & Rejoining the Parauniversity

Voluntary withdrawal follows Section 9. Stopping attendance does not cancel the remaining installments. Rejoining is not automatically guaranteed. Returning students may be evaluated on current technical level, available classes, the current roadmap, and parauniversity requirements at the time of return.

18. Student Acknowledgment

By registering in the parauniversity, the student confirms that they have read, understood, and agreed to follow the parauniversity’s educational system, responsibilities, payment terms, confidentiality requirements, and applicable regulations.
