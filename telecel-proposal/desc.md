Section 03 · Delivery Approach
Delivery approach

Build runs in sequence after kick-off. No Figma design system — only simple general wireframes. Stack and phases below assume kickoff the day after award, with Telecel inputs arriving on the dates in Section 7.

Proposed stack — our preference

The stack below reflects our recommended approach based on delivery speed, maintainability and this team's experience. We are open to Telecel's input: if you prefer a different hosting provider, database, CMS pattern or framework, we can adapt the architecture and revise third-party costs accordingly before kick-off.

Proposed stack
Frontend: Next.js
Hosting / CDN: Vercel Pro
Database: Supabase PostgreSQL
Storage: Supabase Storage
CMS: Custom Next.js admin
Domain: Existing Telecel domain
Email: Zoho Mail (optional)
Logging: Application logging

Phase 01 — Kick-off and wireframes
Confirm scope, sitemap and content inventory against the RFQ's core page list. Simple general wireframes (not Figma). Product owner named and page list frozen.

Phase 02 — Frontend development
Build the public site only in Next.js: page templates, responsive layout, Telecel brand guidelines and portfolio/filter structure. Static templates — not yet connected to the database.

Phase 03 — Admin panel — CRUD and logic
Custom Next.js admin: content types, CRUD flows, roles, forms and the business logic described in the brief — on Supabase and PostgreSQL.

Phase 04 — Connect public pages to the database
Wire the public frontend to live data from Supabase. Populate agreed pages from Telecel-supplied copy and media. SEO fundamentals: meta titles and descriptions, Open Graph tags, sitemap and baseline on-page structure.

Phase 05 — Testing, training, launch and handover
QA across mainstream browsers and priority devices, editor training, go-live on Vercel, and a 30-day warranty window.

Section 08 · Warranty and Maintenance
Warranty terms

For 30 calendar days after go-live (matching the RFQ's own required warranty period), defects in the delivered templates and platform are corrected at no extra fee. A defect is in-scope behaviour accepted at launch, reproduced on a current major browser, and not caused by Telecel edits, third-party outages, or hosting changes outside our control.

Beirut business hours; material defects targeted within two working days
Does not cover new pages, new features, design changes or content writing
Ends if another vendor materially alters production without written agreement

Optional maintenance — quoted separately

Maintenance is a paid retainer for after the 30-day warranty ends: keeping dependencies updated, backups checked, and handling small text/image tweaks. Optional; most sites this size can run without it once Telecel's admins are trained.
